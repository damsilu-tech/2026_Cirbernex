Valley of
the Kings

A hidden landscape built for eternity.

On the west bank of the Nile near ancient Thebes, the Valley of the Kings became the burial place of many New Kingdom rulers. Its tombs preserve extraordinary evidence for royal funerary belief, art and architecture.

Why here?

A landscape
chosen for kings.

The valley's remote desert setting offered privacy and symbolic power. Unlike the pyramids of earlier periods, New Kingdom rulers were buried in rock-cut tombs hidden within a rugged landscape.

The surrounding cliffs and mountain peak created a dramatic setting for a royal necropolis. Tombs were cut into the rock and decorated with religious texts and scenes intended to guide the king through the afterlife.
