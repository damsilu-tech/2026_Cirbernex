Hatshepsut

The woman who ruled as pharaoh.

Hatshepsut was one of ancient Egypt's most successful rulers, remembered for ambitious building projects, long-distance trade and a carefully crafted image of royal authority.

A life in context

A reign built
to last.

Hatshepsut came to power during Egypt's New Kingdom and adopted the full visual language of kingship. Her monuments present her as a legitimate ruler, while inscriptions record a period of trade, construction and religious activity.

Her best-known monument, the temple at Deir el-Bahari, was designed as a dramatic architectural landscape beside the cliffs of western Thebes.
