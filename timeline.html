c. 6000–3100 BCE

Predynastic Egypt

Before the pharaohs, communities along the Nile developed agriculture, craft traditions and trade networks. These regional cultures gradually laid the foundations of one of history's greatest civilisations.

  • First permanent settlements along the Nile Valley
  • Development of agriculture alongside hunting and gathering
  • Regional cultures in Upper and Lower Egypt
  • Early craft traditions — pottery, tools and ornaments

📌 The exact beginning of the Predynastic Period varies among scholars. Date ranges should be treated as broadly defined.

c. 3100–2686 BCE

Early Dynastic Period

The unification of Egypt marks the beginning of pharaonic rule. By around 3100 BCE a unified state had emerged. The first two dynasties established kingship, writing and monumental architecture that defined Egyptian civilisation for millennia.

  • Unification of Upper and Lower Egypt
  • Development of hieroglyphic writing
  • Establishment of Memphis as capital
  • First royal tombs at Abydos

c. 2686–2181 BCE

Old Kingdom

The Age of Pyramids. The Old Kingdom saw monumental architecture, centralised government and artistic achievement. Pharaohs marshalled enormous resources to build the great pyramids of Giza — monuments that still stand today.

  • Construction of the Great Pyramid of Khufu
  • Pharaoh Khafre and the Great Sphinx
  • Development of pyramid texts
  • Highly centralised bureaucratic state
Learn More →
c. 2055–1650 BCE

Middle Kingdom

After a period of fragmentation, Egypt was reunified under Mentuhotep II. The Middle Kingdom brought political stability, artistic refinement and a golden age of literature — often remembered as a period of cultural renewal.

  • Reunification under Mentuhotep II
  • Golden age of Egyptian literature
  • Expansion into Nubia
  • Major irrigation and agricultural projects
Learn More →
c. 1550–1070 BCE

New Kingdom

Egypt's imperial age. The New Kingdom saw Egypt become a dominant international power, producing some of Egypt's most celebrated rulers — Hatshepsut, Akhenaten, Tutankhamun and Ramesses II — and extraordinary monuments.

  • Hatshepsut — Egypt's greatest female pharaoh
  • Akhenaten's religious revolution
  • Tutankhamun and the Valley of the Kings
  • Ramesses II and the Battle of Kadesh
Learn More →
After 1070 BCE

Late Period

Egypt's final chapters. After the New Kingdom, Egypt came under the influence of foreign powers — Libyans, Nubians, Assyrians, Persians and finally the Greeks — until the death of Cleopatra VII in 30 BCE ended three thousand years of pharaonic rule.

  • Third Intermediate Period and division
  • Nubian pharaohs of the 25th Dynasty
  • Persian conquest and occupation
  • Cleopatra VII — the last pharaoh

Key Monuments

Built to last forever.

Ancient Egypt left behind some of the most remarkable structures ever created by human hands.

Great Pyramid of Giza

c. 2560 BCE · Old Kingdom

The only surviving Wonder of the Ancient World — built for Pharaoh Khufu and standing 146 metres tall.

Abu Simbel

c. 1264 BCE · New Kingdom

Two massive rock temples carved by Ramesses II — relocated in the 1960s to save them from rising floodwaters.

Karnak Temple

c. 2055 BCE · Middle Kingdom onward

The largest religious complex ever built — constructed over 2,000 years by successive pharaohs.

Did You Know?

Surprising facts about ancient Egypt.

𓂀

Longest civilisation

Ancient Egypt lasted over 3,000 years — longer than the time between Cleopatra and today.

𓉢

130+ pyramids

There are over 130 pyramids in Egypt — most people only know about the three at Giza.

𓂋

700 hieroglyphs

The Egyptian writing system used over 700 different symbols — and took scholars decades to decipher.

◈

2,000+ gods

Ancient Egyptians worshipped more than 2,000 deities over the course of their civilisation.

♕

Female pharaohs

At least seven women ruled Egypt as pharaoh, including Hatshepsut — one of the most successful rulers in history.

𓀐

Advanced medicine

Egyptian physicians performed surgery, set broken bones and prescribed herbal remedies over 3,500 years ago.
