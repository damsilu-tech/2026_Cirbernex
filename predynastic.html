Early Communities

Who lived in Egypt before the pharaohs?

These were not yet one unified Egyptian nation. They were communities — each with their own pottery styles, burial customs and ways of life — living across the Nile Valley and Delta.

Upper Egypt

Southern Nile Valley

Home to the Naqada culture — known for distinctive decorated pottery, elaborate burial practices and growing political complexity.

  • Naqada I, II & III phases
  • Distinctive red and black pottery
  • Increasingly elaborate graves
Lower Egypt

Northern Nile & Delta

Home to Merimda and Maadi cultures — with strong trade connections to the Near East and their own distinct material traditions.

  • Merimda Beni Salama settlement
  • Maadi culture near Cairo
  • Strong Near Eastern connections

🧭 Over time, contact between these regions increased — helping create the cultural and political connections that eventually led to unification.

Archaeologists' Evidence

How do we know all this?

We have no written records from the Predynastic Period. Everything we know comes from objects left behind — and what archaeologists have been able to read from them.

🏺

Pottery

What it tells us:
  • Technology and firing techniques
  • Artistic traditions and style
  • Daily activities and food storage
  • Connections between communities
⚱️

Graves

What they tell us:
  • Burial customs and rituals
  • Beliefs about death and afterlife
  • Social differences and status
  • How practices changed over time
🪨

Stone Tools

What they tell us:
  • How people hunted and farmed
  • Craft skills and techniques
  • Practical daily life
  • Changes in technology over time
💎

Ornaments

What they tell us:
  • Craftsmanship and skill
  • Personal and social identity
  • Trade routes and connections
  • Movement of valuable materials
Trade & Connections

Connected across Africa.

Materials found far from their likely sources show that Predynastic communities were already part of wide exchange networks stretching across northeastern Africa.

Obsidian

Volcanic glass from Ethiopia — prized for making sharp blades.

Lapis Lazuli

Deep blue stone from Afghanistan — a symbol of wealth and status.

Red Sea Shells

Shells used as ornaments appear far inland — evidence of long-distance movement.

Ivory & Cedar

Materials from sub-Saharan Africa and the Levant found in Predynastic graves.

Exchange networks connected communities across the ancient world
Burial practices reveal ideas about death and identity
Beliefs & Burials

What the graves tell us.

We have no written records of Predynastic beliefs. But graves tell their own story. Objects placed with the dead — pottery, tools, ornaments — suggest people believed in some form of existence after death.

01
Simple pit graves

Bodies placed in shallow desert pits — preserved naturally by dry sand.

02
Grave goods appear

Pottery, tools and ornaments placed with the dead — suggesting belief in an afterlife.

03
Elaborate burials

Larger graves, wooden linings and more objects — showing growing social differences.

Beliefs were not uniform — they varied across regions and changed over thousands of years.
