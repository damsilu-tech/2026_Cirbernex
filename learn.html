03 / Pharaohs & Kings

The People Who
Ruled Egypt.

For thousands of years, Egypt was ruled by kings who were later known as pharaohs. The pharaoh stood at the centre of Egypt's political and religious system, responsible for maintaining order and protecting the kingdom. But behind the royal image were real people with ambitions, challenges, families and legacies.

What Was a Pharaoh? — More Than a King

The Egyptian ruler was both a political leader and an important religious figure. Kingship was closely connected with maintaining maat — the principle of order, balance and justice.

👑

Ruler

Led the kingdom and its administration.

⚖️

Guardian of Order

Was expected to maintain maat.

🛕

Religious Leader

Played a central role in Egypt's relationship with the gods.

How Did Kingship Work? — A Kingdom Built Around the Crown

  • The crown was passed through royal succession, although succession could be complicated.
  • Royal families played an important role in maintaining dynastic power.
  • Kings were supported by officials, priests, scribes, soldiers and administrators.
  • Pharaohs commissioned temples, monuments and statues that communicated their authority.
  • Different dynasties and periods brought major changes to Egypt.

A World Shaped
by the Gods.

Religion was deeply woven into ancient Egyptian life. Egyptians believed that gods and goddesses influenced the natural world, protected communities, supported kingship and played important roles in the journey after death. Their beliefs changed and developed over thousands of years.

What Did Egyptians Believe? — Keeping the World in Balance

Egyptians valued maat, a concept associated with order, balance, justice and harmony. The king, priests and society were expected to help maintain this order.

05 / Hieroglyphs

The Language of
Ancient Egypt.

Ancient Egyptian writing was used for religious texts, royal inscriptions, administration and many other purposes. Hieroglyphs combine signs representing sounds, words and ideas, creating a writing system that could be both practical and highly artistic.

What Are Hieroglyphs? — Writing in Pictures

Hieroglyphs are signs used in the writing of ancient Egyptian. Some signs represented sounds, while others represented words or helped clarify their meaning.

𓆓sound
𓉔sound
𓂋sound
𓇳word / idea

How Do Hieroglyphs Work? — Sounds, Words & Meaning

🔊

Phonograms

Signs that represent sounds.

💡

Ideograms

Signs that can represent an idea or word.

📝

Determinatives

Signs that help clarify the meaning of a word.

A single hieroglyph does not always work like a modern letter. Understanding Egyptian writing means looking at how signs work together.

Which Way Do You Read? — Direction Matters

Hieroglyphic writing could be arranged in different directions. The direction the human or animal figures face can help indicate where the text begins.

06 / Pyramids & Monuments

Monuments That
Still Speak.

Across thousands of years, Egyptians built pyramids, temples, tombs and other monumental structures that transformed the landscape. These places were connected to kingship, religion, death, memory and community — and they provide some of the clearest evidence we have for understanding ancient Egyptian civilization.

Why Were Pyramids Built? — More Than Giant Structures

Pyramids were monumental tombs associated with Egyptian kingship and the afterlife. Their design, location and construction reflected the religious beliefs and political organization of their time.

👑

Kingship

Monuments reinforced the importance and legacy of the ruler.

⚱️

Afterlife

Pyramids formed part of royal funerary complexes.

🏗️

Organization

Their construction required planning, resources, skilled workers and administration.

07 / Daily Life

Beyond the
Pharaohs.

Ancient Egypt was not only about pharaohs, pyramids and gods. Most Egyptians were farmers, craftsmen, scribes, servants, priests, soldiers and other workers — the communities that kept Egyptian society functioning.

Life Along the Nile

Farming depended on the river and its flood cycle. Villages and cities developed along the Nile Valley, which was important for transportation, fishing, farming and trade — especially given the dry desert surrounding it.

Nile
Food
Work
Trade

Food & Cooking

Bread and beer were common staples, alongside vegetables, fruits, grains, fish and meat. Food preparation, storage and access varied between social groups.

BreadBeer VegetablesFruits GrainsFish Meat

Homes & Communities

Houses varied according to wealth and location, and could contain courtyards, storage areas, living spaces and work areas. Mudbrick was an important building material, and villages and cities created communities around work, family, religion and trade.

Work & Professions

Egyptian society depended on many different kinds of workers — not just rulers.

Family & Childhood

Life began with family and community. Evidence points to family relationships, childhood, education for some children, household responsibilities and marriage — though ancient family life should not be assumed to mirror modern life; we rely on what the evidence actually tells us.

Clothing & Appearance

Linen was widely used, and clothing styles varied with status, occupation and period. Jewellery and cosmetics could have practical, social, or symbolic roles.

Education & Scribes

Writing was a specialized skill. Scribes trained to use hieroglyphs and other Egyptian writing systems to produce administrative records and religious and funerary texts.

Physicians existed as specialized professionals, and Egyptians recorded medical knowledge. Treatments combined practical remedies with religious or magical beliefs, and surviving medical papyri provide evidence about ancient healthcare.
