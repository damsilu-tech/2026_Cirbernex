The Language of Ancient Egypt

Hieroglyphs

Hieroglyphs used signs to represent sounds, words and ideas. Egyptian writing could combine phonograms, ideograms and determinatives, and its direction could vary — human and animal figures generally face toward the start of a line, helping readers know which way to read. Egyptians wrote on materials including stone and papyrus, and scribes trained for years to master the system.

The People Who Ruled Egypt

Pharaohs & Kings

A pharaoh was not simply a political ruler. Egyptian kingship was connected with religion, society and maintaining maat. Around the throne stood royal families, officials, priests, scribes and soldiers — together responsible for administration, worship and the great monument-building projects that still stand today.

👑

Hatshepsut

One of the most successful female pharaohs, known for trade expeditions and monumental building.
👑

Tutankhamun

The boy pharaoh whose nearly intact tomb transformed our understanding of royal burial practices.
👑

Ramesses II

A long-reigning New Kingdom pharaoh remembered for monumental temples and military campaigns.
👑

Cleopatra VII

The last active ruler of the Ptolemaic Kingdom, at the crossroads of Egyptian and Mediterranean history.

Egyptian history includes many important people beyond rulers — officials, priests, scribes, artisans and everyday families all shaped the civilization.

A World Shaped by the Gods

Gods & Beliefs

Ancient Egyptian religion was diverse and changed considerably over thousands of years, with different deities rising in importance across regions and periods.

☀

Ra

Sun god associated with kingship and daily renewal.

𓋹

𓆇

𓃠

What Is Maat?

Maat represented order, balance, justice and harmony — a concept central to kingship, law and religious life.

Temples & Worship

Temples served as homes for the gods, where priests performed daily rituals on behalf of the community.

Rituals & Offerings

Offerings of food, incense and objects were important parts of maintaining a relationship with the gods.

Death & Afterlife

Beliefs about the afterlife connected directly to tombs, funerary objects and religious texts.

Gods & Pharaohs

Kingship and religion were deeply connected — the pharaoh was expected to uphold maat on behalf of the gods.

Monuments That Still Speak

Pyramids & Monuments

Monuments provide evidence about kingship, religion, engineering, organization, craftsmanship, funerary practices and society at large.

Why Were Pyramids Built?

Pyramids were royal funerary complexes connected to beliefs about kingship and the afterlife. Exact construction methods remain an active area of ongoing archaeological research.

The Pyramids of Giza

Built for Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure, the Giza pyramids remain the most iconic monuments of the Old Kingdom.

Temples

Sites such as Karnak, Luxor, Abu Simbel and Deir el-Bahari reveal how worship and monumental architecture developed over time.

Tombs & Necropolises

The Valley of the Kings holds the tombs of many New Kingdom pharaohs, hidden away from the great pyramids of earlier eras.

How Ordinary Egyptians Lived

Daily Life

Ancient Egypt was not only pharaohs and pyramids. Most Egyptians were farmers, craftspeople and laborers whose lives varied by region, period and social standing.

Life Along the Nile

The river's annual flood shaped farming cycles, settlement and travel.

Food & Cooking

Bread, beer, vegetables and fish formed the basis of the everyday diet.

Homes & Communities

Mudbrick homes clustered into villages and towns along the fertile land.

Work & Professions

Egyptians worked as farmers, craftspeople, scribes and officials, among many other roles.

Family & Childhood

Family life centered the household, with children learning trades from parents.

Clothing & Appearance

Linen clothing, jewelry and cosmetics reflected status and practicality in the climate.

Education & Scribes

Formal education was limited mostly to training future scribes and officials.

Music, Games & Health

Music, board games and early medical knowledge were all part of daily life.
