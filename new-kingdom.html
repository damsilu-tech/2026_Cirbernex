The Rise of a New Egypt

Reunification and a new dynasty.

After the Second Intermediate Period, Egypt was reunified under rulers of the early 18th Dynasty. The New Kingdom began around 1550 BCE and lasted until approximately 1070 BCE. During this period, Egypt became one of the major powers of the ancient Near East.

⚖️

The pharaoh was not only the ruler of Egypt but also a central religious figure, responsible for maintaining ma'at — the principle of cosmic order, justice and balance that underpinned the entire New Kingdom state.

Egypt Becomes an Empire

A kingdom reaches outward.

This is one of the most important differences between New Kingdom Egypt and the earlier periods. Egyptian rulers expanded their influence southward into Nubia and northward into the Levant, while the Nile Valley remained the political and religious heart of the state.

Key Pharaohs

The rulers who built an empire.

Several remarkable pharaohs defined the New Kingdom — from a female king who prioritised trade and monuments, to a religious revolutionary, to Egypt's most famous boy king and its greatest builder.

18th Dynasty
👑

Hatshepsut

One of Egypt's most successful female rulers. She emphasised trade, monumental construction and peaceful prosperity. Her mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari is one of the defining monuments of her reign.

Known from: Deir el-Bahari temple · Punt reliefs
18th Dynasty
⚔️

Thutmose III

Often remembered for his military campaigns and expansion of Egyptian influence. His reign helped establish Egypt as a dominant regional power.

Known from: Annals at Karnak · Military campaigns
18th Dynasty
☀️

Akhenaten

Introduced a dramatic religious transformation centred on the Aten. He founded a new royal city, Akhetaten, at modern Amarna.

Known from: Amarna city ruins · Amarna art
18th Dynasty
𓋹

Tutankhamun

A young pharaoh whose relatively short reign became famous worldwide because his tomb was discovered with a remarkably rich collection of objects.

Known from: KV62 tomb · Funerary mask
19th Dynasty
🏛️

Ramesses II

One of the most famous pharaohs of ancient Egypt. His reign was marked by extensive building projects, military campaigns and monumental inscriptions.

Known from: Abu Simbel · Ramesseum
20th Dynasty
🛡️

Ramesses III

One of the last great rulers of the New Kingdom, known for defending Egypt during a period of major external pressures.

Known from: Medinet Habu temple

📌 Our knowledge of these rulers comes from archaeological evidence including monuments, inscriptions and tomb contents. Exact dates continue to be refined by scholars.

The Valley of the Kings

Where pharaohs were hidden.

During the New Kingdom, many royal burials moved away from enormous pyramids toward hidden rock-cut tombs in the western Theban landscape. The Valley of the Kings became the burial place of numerous New Kingdom rulers.

Rather than simply saying pharaohs were buried here, it's worth asking why this hidden valley became Egypt's royal necropolis for nearly 500 years.

🛡️
Protection

Hidden tombs reduced the visibility of royal burials and made them harder to locate and rob.

🌅
Religion

The western horizon was associated with the setting sun and the realm of the dead.

🎨
Art

Tomb walls became richly decorated with religious texts and scenes intended to guide the deceased through the afterlife.

The western Theban hills became the hidden burial ground of New Kingdom pharaohs
Temples of the New Kingdom

Monuments that transformed the landscape.

Pharaohs of the New Kingdom built on a scale never seen before — vast temple complexes dedicated to the gods and to their own memory.

Karnak

A vast religious complex dedicated especially to Amun and developed by generations of rulers.

Luxor Temple

A major temple associated with kingship, religious festivals and the renewal of royal power.

Deir el-Bahari

The spectacular mortuary temple of Hatshepsut, built against the cliffs of western Thebes.

Abu Simbel

The monumental temples commissioned by Ramesses II, carved into the rock of southern Egypt.

Religion & the Aten

The Amarna revolution.

Under Akhenaten, royal religion changed dramatically. The Aten — the visible disk of the sun — became the focus of royal worship, and Akhenaten established a new capital at Akhetaten. After his death, Egypt gradually returned to traditional religious practices.

An empire built by people.

The New Kingdom wasn't only about kings. Soldiers, scribes, craftspeople, farmers and priests made the imperial state function day to day.

⚔️

✍️

🙏

New Kingdom Art

What changed?

New Kingdom art is famous for its highly detailed representations of royalty, gods, religious rituals, offerings, warfare, nature, daily life and the afterlife.

Art was not decoration alone. It was part of a religious and social system designed to preserve order, identity and memory.

The End of the New Kingdom

An empire enters a new era.

By the later New Kingdom, Egypt faced increasing political, economic and social pressures. The New Kingdom had transformed Egypt into a major regional power, but its political and economic system could not remain unchanged forever. After the 20th Dynasty, Egypt entered the Third Intermediate Period, with political authority becoming more divided.
