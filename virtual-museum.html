Room 01

Origins

Before the pharaohs — the Predynastic communities that built the foundations of Egypt.

Long before the pyramids, communities along the Nile Valley developed farming, craft traditions and social structures that would eventually become one of history's greatest civilisations. This room explores the foundations — the objects, communities and ideas that came before the pharaohs.

Room 02

Age of Pyramids

The Old Kingdom — monumental architecture, centralised kingship, and the world's most iconic structures.

The Old Kingdom (c. 2686–2181 BCE) produced some of Egypt's most enduring achievements — including the Great Pyramid of Giza, still standing after 4,500 years. This room explores the monuments, objects and ideas of Egypt's most architecturally ambitious era.

Explore the Old Kingdom →

The Great Pyramid of Khufu

c. 2560 BCE · Old Kingdom

The only surviving Wonder of the Ancient World. Built for Pharaoh Khufu of the 4th Dynasty, the Great Pyramid stands 146 metres tall and is constructed from approximately 2.3 million stone blocks.

Room 03

Gods & Beliefs

Ancient Egyptians worshipped over 2,000 deities — each connected with different aspects of life, nature and the cosmos.

Religion was woven through every aspect of ancient Egyptian life. Gods were present in the floods of the Nile, the movement of the sun, the harvest, birth and death. This room introduces some of the most important deities and the objects connected with Egyptian religious practice.

Room 05

Life on the Nile

A kingdom was built by more than its rulers — discover the lives of ordinary Egyptians.

Behind the temples and tombs, millions of people lived along the Nile — farming its floodplains, crafting its objects, raising families and building the civilisation that still fascinates us today. This room honours those whose names were rarely recorded.

A Day on the Nile · 01 of 06

Farming

The annual Nile flood deposited fertile silt, enabling Egyptian farmers to grow wheat, barley and flax year after year.

A Day on the Nile · 02 of 06

Crafts

Potters, weavers, stonemasons and metalworkers produced both everyday objects and treasures for the royal court.

A Day on the Nile · 03 of 06

Writing

Scribes were among the most respected members of Egyptian society — their ability to read and write gave them access to power.

A Day on the Nile · 04 of 06

Fishing

The Nile provided fish as a vital food source for communities from the Delta to Upper Egypt throughout the year.

A Day on the Nile · 05 of 06

Communities

People lived in villages and towns connected by the river — the Nile was Egypt's highway, marketplace and lifeline.

A Day on the Nile · 06 of 06

Trade

Goods moved across Egypt and beyond — connecting communities and supplying the royal court with materials from across the ancient world.

Room 06

The Afterlife

Ancient Egyptians believed death was not an ending — but a passage to another existence.

Egyptian beliefs about death and the afterlife were among the most elaborate in the ancient world. This room explores the traditions, objects and beliefs surrounding death — from mummification to the Book of the Dead — approached with educational respect.

Explore Funerary Collection →
Chapter 01 of 04

Mummification

The body was preserved through a 70-day process involving the removal of organs, drying with natron salt, and careful wrapping in linen bandages.

Chapter 02 of 04

The Book of the Dead

A collection of spells to help the deceased navigate the dangers of the underworld — placed in the tomb to guide the dead on their journey.

Chapter 03 of 04

The Weighing of the Heart

In the Hall of Two Truths, the deceased's heart was weighed against the feather of Ma'at. A light heart meant entrance to the Field of Reeds — the Egyptian paradise.

Chapter 04 of 04

The Field of Reeds

The ideal afterlife — a perfect reflection of Egyptian life along the Nile, where the deceased could live in peace, tend fields and be reunited with loved ones.
