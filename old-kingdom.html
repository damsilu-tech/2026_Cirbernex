A Continuing Story

From Early Dynastic Egypt to the Old Kingdom

The political and administrative foundations established during the Early Dynastic Period continued to develop during the Old Kingdom. Egyptian kings commanded increasingly ambitious royal projects, while religious and administrative institutions became more elaborate.

The Pharaoh

What made Old Kingdom kingship different.

  • Political ruler
  • Religious authority
  • Leader of the state
  • Patron of temples and monuments
  • Central figure in royal funerary traditions

The king stood at the center of Egyptian political and religious life.

Egyptians associated kingship with maintaining ma'at — a concept involving order, balance and the proper functioning of the world and society.

Step Pyramid of Djoser

The Step Pyramid complex at Saqqara represents a major development in Egyptian monumental architecture. It was built for King Djoser and is traditionally associated with the architect Imhotep.

How Were the Pyramids Built?

What archaeology tells us.

Visitors will almost certainly ask "how did they build them?" There's no single secret method — evidence points to organized, large-scale state projects.

01Quarrying stone
02Transporting materials
03Organizing workers
04Food and supplies
05Construction planning
06Engineering
07Administration

Evidence — including workers' settlements found near Giza — supports large, organized workforces of laborers and skilled crews, rather than the simplistic idea that the pyramids were built only by enslaved people.

Pyramid Texts

Words carved for the afterlife.

Some Old Kingdom pyramids contain religious texts inscribed on their interior walls. These texts are among the earliest large bodies of ancient Egyptian religious writing known today.

They contain spells and religious material connected with the king's afterlife and protection — giving the Old Kingdom something to offer beyond architecture alone.

Art & Tombs

More than decoration.

Old Kingdom art represented kings, gods, offerings, farming, hunting, family and daily activities.

Tomb scenes were not simply decoration — they could have religious and symbolic purposes connected with the deceased.

The End of the Old Kingdom

A kingdom changes.

Rather than one single cause, historians discuss multiple factors behind the end of the Old Kingdom — including political changes, economic pressures, environmental conditions and changing relationships between the central government and regional officials.
