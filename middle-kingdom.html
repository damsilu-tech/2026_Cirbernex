From Fragmentation to Unity

How Egypt came back together.

The Old Kingdom ended with a period of political fragmentation — the First Intermediate Period. The Middle Kingdom begins with the reunification of Egypt under Mentuhotep II of the 11th Dynasty, who brought the country under centralised control once again.

Middle Kingdom Egypt enters a new golden age

Mentuhotep II is often considered one of the most important kings in Egyptian history — the pharaoh who ended the First Intermediate Period and brought Egypt together. His mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari in Thebes became a major monument of the period.

Key Pharaohs

The rulers who shaped the Middle Kingdom.

Several important pharaohs defined the Middle Kingdom — each contributing to the political, cultural and religious development of Egypt.

11th Dynasty
𓂀

Mentuhotep II

The reunifier of Egypt — ended the First Intermediate Period and established the Middle Kingdom. Built a significant mortuary complex at Deir el-Bahari, later echoed by Hatshepsut's temple nearby.

Known from: Deir el-Bahari temple · Royal inscriptions
12th Dynasty
♕

Amenemhat I

Founded the 12th Dynasty — often seen as the peak of the Middle Kingdom. Moved the capital to Itj-Tawy near the Fayum and introduced new administrative approaches.

Known from: Literary texts · Royal monuments
12th Dynasty
𓉢

Senusret I

Son of Amenemhat I — known for building projects across Egypt and into Nubia, and for a reign that produced significant literary and artistic achievements.

Known from: White Chapel, Karnak · Nubian fortresses
12th Dynasty
◈

Senusret III

One of the most powerful 12th Dynasty kings — known for military campaigns into Nubia, administrative reforms and distinctive portrait statues showing a more realistic royal image.

Known from: Nubian fortresses · Portrait statues
12th Dynasty
𓂋

Amenemhat III

One of the longest-reigning kings of the Middle Kingdom — associated with major agricultural and irrigation projects in the Fayum region and significant building activity.

Known from: Fayum projects · Pyramid at Hawara
12th Dynasty
𓏏

Sobekneferu

One of the few women to have ruled Egypt as pharaoh — the last ruler of the 12th Dynasty. Known from statues and inscriptions, her reign marks the end of the 12th Dynasty's golden age.

Known from: Royal statues · Inscriptions

📌 Our knowledge of these rulers comes from archaeological evidence including monuments, inscriptions, literary texts and tomb contents. Exact dates continue to be refined by scholars.

Kingship in the Middle Kingdom

A more complex royal image.

Middle Kingdom royal imagery sometimes showed pharaohs differently from earlier periods — including portrait statues with more individualised, sometimes careworn faces. This has been interpreted by some scholars as reflecting a changed conception of kingship — the king as a shepherd responsible for his people.

The king remained a divine figure — but the relationship between king, gods and people became a more prominent theme in Middle Kingdom texts and artistic traditions.

👑
Divine Authority

The king remained the central political and religious figure of the Egyptian state.

🐑
Shepherd King

Some Middle Kingdom texts present the king as a protector and guardian of his people.

⚖️
Ma'at

Maintaining order, balance and justice remained a central obligation of kingship.

Middle Kingdom royal imagery sometimes showed more individualised features
Administration & Government

Running a reunified kingdom.

The Middle Kingdom saw significant developments in how Egypt was governed — including reforms to the role of regional officials and the development of a more complex administrative system.

👑

Pharaoh

Central authority — political ruler, religious figure and guarantor of ma'at across the reunified state.

📋

Vizier

The highest administrative official below the pharaoh — responsible for managing the state's day-to-day affairs.

🏛️

Officials

A growing network of state officials — managing resources, records, construction projects and local administration.

✍️

Scribes

Scribes recorded information, managed resources and maintained the written record that held the administration together.

🏘️

Local Governors

Regional officials (nomarchs) managed local territories — their relationship with central government changed during the Middle Kingdom.

⚖️

Justice

Legal processes and dispute resolution were part of the state administration — connected with the concept of ma'at.

Literature

A golden age of Egyptian writing.

The Middle Kingdom is sometimes called the classical age of Egyptian literature. A number of significant literary works were composed during this period — and later Egyptians looked back on this era as a high point of Egyptian culture and language.

📖

Story of Sinuhe

One of Egypt's most celebrated literary works — the story of an Egyptian official who flees to Canaan after the death of a king, lives abroad for years, and eventually returns to Egypt. It explores themes of identity, loyalty and belonging.

📜

Eloquent Peasant

A narrative in which a peasant delivers eloquent speeches appealing for justice against an official who has wronged him — exploring the theme of ma'at and the ideal of good governance.

🗣️

Teachings of Amenemhat

A literary text in which Amenemhat I appears to advise his son from beyond death — exploring themes of kingship, loyalty and the nature of royal authority.

𓂋

Hymn to the Nile

A hymn celebrating the Nile flood as the source of Egypt's life and prosperity — an important example of Middle Kingdom religious and literary tradition.

🏺

Coffin Texts

Religious spells written on coffins — an evolution from the Pyramid Texts of the Old Kingdom, now more widely available to the elite rather than only royalty.

📝

Didactic Literature

Instructions and wisdom texts offering advice on proper conduct — an important genre that flourished during the Middle Kingdom.

📌 The texts listed here are among those attributed to the Middle Kingdom, though the transmission and dating of ancient Egyptian literature involves scholarly complexity and debate.
Middle Kingdom art is noted for its distinctive style and craftsmanship
Art & Sculpture

A distinctive artistic tradition.

Middle Kingdom art is often distinguished by a new approach to royal portraiture — including statues showing more individualised, sometimes austere royal faces. Tomb decoration and relief carving continued to develop alongside new artistic forms.

🗿
Royal Statuary

Portrait statues of pharaohs — sometimes showing more realistic features than earlier royal sculpture.

🪨
Relief Carving

Carved relief scenes in temples and tombs — continuing earlier Egyptian artistic conventions with new refinements.

🏺
Pottery & Crafts

Fine pottery, jewellery and crafted objects from elite tombs and settlements across Egypt.

📿
Jewellery

Elaborately crafted jewellery — some of the finest examples of ancient Egyptian goldwork survive from the Middle Kingdom.

Religion & Beliefs

The gods in the Middle Kingdom.

Religious practice continued to develop during the Middle Kingdom — with Osiris becoming increasingly prominent as a god connected with the afterlife and resurrection.

⚱️

Osiris

God of the afterlife and resurrection — became increasingly important during the Middle Kingdom. The Osiris cult connected beliefs about death, resurrection and the continuation of life.

𓆣

Amun

Amun — later known as Amun-Ra — grew in prominence as a major deity associated with Thebes, which became an important royal and religious centre during the Middle Kingdom.

🐊

Sobek

The crocodile god became particularly important during the Middle Kingdom — especially in the Fayum region, which was connected with major royal projects of this era.

📜

Coffin Texts

Religious spells for the afterlife — previously the domain of royalty — became more widely available to the elite through the Coffin Texts, written on coffins rather than pyramid walls.

𓂀

Abydos

Abydos remained an important religious centre — connected with Osiris and the cult of the dead. Many Egyptians sought to be buried near or to make a pilgrimage to Abydos.

⚖️

Ma'at

The principle of order, balance and justice remained central to Egyptian religious and political thought — expressed through royal texts, tomb scenes and funerary practice.

Nubia & Expansion

Egypt looks south.

The Middle Kingdom saw significant Egyptian activity in Nubia — to the south of Egypt — including military campaigns and the construction of a series of fortresses along the Nile to control movement and trade.

Military Campaigns

Several Middle Kingdom pharaohs led campaigns into Nubia — most notably Senusret III, who pushed Egyptian control further south.

Nubian Fortresses

A series of fortresses was constructed along the Nile — controlling movement, protecting trade routes and projecting Egyptian power.

Trade & Resources

Nubia was a source of gold, stone and other resources important to the Egyptian state. Control of Nubia gave Egypt access to these materials.

Cultural Exchange

Egyptian and Nubian cultures had long been in contact — objects, artistic traditions and ideas moved in both directions across the border region.

📌 Egypt's relationship with Nubia was complex — involving trade, diplomacy and military control at different times and places. The nature of these connections continues to be studied by archaeologists and historians.
The Nile connected Egypt and Nubia across thousands of kilometres
Daily Life

A kingdom built by people.

Behind the royal monuments and literary achievements, millions of ordinary Egyptians lived and worked along the Nile — their lives the foundation on which the Middle Kingdom was built.

🌾

Farming

Agricultural production remained the foundation of the Egyptian economy — the Nile flood renewed the soil and enabled crops to be grown year after year.

🏺

Crafts

Skilled craftspeople produced pottery, textiles, jewellery and other goods — both for everyday use and as luxury items for the elite and royal court.

✍️

Scribes

Scribes were among the most respected members of Egyptian society — their literacy gave them access to administrative, religious and political roles.

🏘️

Towns & Villages

People lived in communities along the Nile — evidence from sites like Kahun gives us a picture of what Egyptian town life looked like during the Middle Kingdom.

⚕️

Medicine

Middle Kingdom medical papyri survive — among the earliest evidence of Egyptian medical knowledge, including treatments for injuries and conditions.

🎶

Music & Leisure

Tomb scenes depict music, dance, games and feasting — suggesting that leisure and entertainment were part of life for at least the Egyptian elite.

Kahun — A Window into Middle Kingdom Life

Kahun (Lahun) was a town built to house workers associated with the pyramid complex of Amenemhat II. Archaeological excavations at Kahun have produced papyri, tools and everyday objects — giving archaeologists a rare picture of organised town life during the Middle Kingdom.

Trade & Connections

Egypt reaches outward.

Middle Kingdom Egypt maintained connections with neighbouring regions — through trade, diplomatic exchange and the movement of materials, objects and ideas.

Punt

Expeditions to Punt brought back myrrh, incense, ebony, ivory and other exotic goods.

Sinai

Egyptian expeditions to Sinai sought turquoise and copper — important materials for craft production.

Levant

Connections with the Levant — including trade in silver, cedar wood and other materials not available in Egypt.

Nubia

Gold, stone and other resources from Nubia were important to the Egyptian state economy.

📌 Trade claims should be verified against museum and academic sources. The nature and extent of Middle Kingdom connections continue to be studied.

Trade goods connected Egypt with regions across the ancient world
Women in the Middle Kingdom

Queens, officials and everyday women.

Women held a range of roles in Middle Kingdom Egyptian society — from queens and royal women to scribes, craftspeople and farmers. The evidence for women's lives comes from tombs, legal documents, literary texts and everyday objects.

👑

Queens

Royal women played important religious and political roles — some are known from significant monuments and inscriptions of their own.

𓏏

Sobekneferu

The last ruler of the 12th Dynasty — one of the few women in Egyptian history to have ruled as pharaoh in her own right.

📜

Legal Rights

Evidence from papyri suggests Egyptian women could own property, make contracts and participate in legal proceedings.

🌾

Everyday Life

Most women lived as farmers, weavers, brewers and household managers — their labour was fundamental to the Egyptian economy.

How Do We Know?

Reconstructing the Middle Kingdom.

Historians and archaeologists reconstruct the Middle Kingdom from a rich range of surviving evidence — including some of the most important literary texts from ancient Egypt.

📖

Literary Texts

Stories, teachings and hymns — providing evidence of language, culture, values and ideas during the Middle Kingdom.

🏛️

Monuments

Temples, tombs and royal monuments — including Mentuhotep II's temple at Deir el-Bahari and the White Chapel at Karnak.

⚱️

Tombs

Royal and elite burials with inscriptions, objects and decorated chambers — providing evidence of belief, status and material culture.

📜

Papyri

Administrative and medical papyri — including those from Kahun — providing direct evidence of everyday life and administrative practice.

🏺

Artifacts

Pottery, jewellery, tools and everyday objects from tombs, settlements and storage areas across Egypt and beyond.

🏘️

Settlements

Sites like Kahun provide rare evidence of everyday town life — what people ate, wore, used and believed in the Middle Kingdom.

The End of the Middle Kingdom

Another period of change.

The Middle Kingdom came to an end with another period of political fragmentation — the Second Intermediate Period. This was also the period when the Hyksos, a people from the Near East, established control over parts of Lower Egypt. Rather than one single cause, historians discuss multiple factors behind the end of the Middle Kingdom.
